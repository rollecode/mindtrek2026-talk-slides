## Speaker notes

### 3. Sovereignty as a strategy, or as a habit

The conference theme is Building Europe's Digital Sovereignty. This talk looks at it from the daily work of one small agency and its servers.

### 4. Layers we rarely look inside

These layers solve real problems, and I use some of them. For 20 years my preference has been different: build from source, and run things on servers I can log into, read and change.

### 5. Where the habit comes from

My father had an Amstrad CPC, then 386 and 486 machines. Linux was in the house early, and a server I could log into became normal long before I had a word for why it mattered. [Check: the year of the first own server, which the "20 years" in the title rests on.]

### 6. Dude 2013-2015: from partners to our own server

I wanted a purely command-line way of working. The first VPS ran HHVM, which kept crashing, so I wrote a cron script that restarted it. That was the start of doing operations ourselves.

### 7. Choosing providers, and leaving them

One Monday morning every client site hung, and nobody in France could be reached. Weeks later a report arrived: an excavator had cut the network. We moved every site to Multim in one night in October 2016. The old DigitalOcean server we kept for legacy sites is still called Monday. [Check: the 2016 blog post describes weeks of slow nights before the move; settle which version to tell.]

### 8. The fleet today

Deploys go from Git with Capistrano. Larger sites get a cluster with HAProxy, Galera and lsyncd. The self-hosted services are Plausible, Outline, Twenty and mementomori.social.

### 9. From source and from upstream

The title says from source, so to be exact: most software comes from upstream, and a few modules are compiled by us. What matters is unmodified upstream software, configured by hand, on a server we can read.

### 10. When a layer breaks

This is the whole talk in one incident. The crash was a segfault inside a module. Knowing how nginx and its modules are built is what found the cause.

### 11. Automation, and what stays manual

Ansible is something I used to do only with a gun to my head. Lately I have grown fond of it.

### 14. Where it still depends on others

Self-hosting does not make a company independent of everyone. These are the dependencies we keep on purpose, and knowing them is part of the work.

### 15. Knowing every layer is practical sovereignty

Knowing every layer of your stack is the most practical form of digital sovereignty a developer can have. One concrete first step for the developers and students in the room.
